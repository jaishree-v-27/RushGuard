RushGuard

Fair Access to Academic Web Forms: Complete Project Document

A session-aware rate-limiting and fairness monitor that gently throttles script floods while keeping forms open for legitimate, concurrent students. It is explainable, reversible and AI-assisted.

1. Project at a Glance

Item Detail

2. The Problem

2.1 Context

Students and staff use academic web services to fill in forms, open materials and submit work. These services run on shared capacity: every request consumes a slice of what everyone else needs.

2.2 What goes wrong

A script (a retry loop, an auto-refresher or deliberate hammering) can repeat submissions many times per second from one session. This causes slower responses for others, unfair use of capacity, and operators who cannot see who is responsible or why.

2.3 Why it is hard

Different situations create heavy traffic, and a simple counter cannot separate them:

Situation Pattern
Genuine rush (deadline night) Many different sessions, about one submit each
Script flood One session, many submits in seconds
Slow-drip script One session, steady submits spaced just under a short limit
Nervous student One person, a double-click or a couple of retries

Blocking everyone who looks busy punishes the innocent majority, and a wrongly blocked student may miss a deadline.

2.4 Why common fixes fall short

  • Hard blocks and error pages are punishing and hard to undo.
  • Global rate limits slow everyone during a rush.
  • Invasive detection (IP tracking, fingerprinting, mouse or keystroke monitoring) is inappropriate for a student service.
  • Opaque scoring gives operators no reason to trust a decision.
  • A single short window is easy to evade by spacing requests out.

2.5 Problem statement

Build a monitor that separates many people submitting once from one session submitting repeatedly, including slow-drip evasion. It must respond proportionately and reversibly, explain every decision in plain language, protect every student's submission time, and prove its impact, all without real accounts, IP addresses or invasive fingerprinting.

3. Objectives

  1. Detect rapid and slow-drip repeated submissions per session using two windows.
  2. Never treat a busy period of distinct sessions as a flood.
  3. Respond in graded steps: Allow, Warn, temporary Slow. Never lock anyone out.
  4. Make every decision reversible by an operator or the AI operator, with a record.
  5. Explain every decision with the threshold that fired and recent requests.
  6. Guarantee submission timestamps even when slowed.
  7. Prove impact with a Guard OFF vs ON benchmark.
  8. Make behaviour visible at a glance with the Traffic Barcode.
  9. Use AI to explain, advise and assist, within strict guardrails.
  10. Demonstrate normal, busy and scripted behaviour in two minutes.

4. Scope and Boundaries

In scope: a local mock form, replay of generated traffic, a rule-based detector, an operator dashboard, an AI assistant panel, three scenarios.

Out of scope, by design

  • Real accounts, IP addresses, production portals or real student data.
  • Browser fingerprinting, mouse or keystroke tracking, or any invasive telemetry.
  • Machine-learning classifiers for the core decision, or large datasets.
  • Defence against attackers who rotate sessions or run distributed attacks. RushGuard is a fairness aid, not a security product.

5. Solution Overview: Three Layers

  1. Rule engine (deterministic): counts submissions, applies thresholds, produces decisions. Instant, predictable, explainable.
  2. Visual and impact layer: Traffic Barcode, Guard OFF vs ON benchmark, live graph and fairness scorecard.
  3. AI assistant layer: turns engine output into explanations, suggestions and, optionally, bounded actions.

The AI sits beside the rule engine, never inside it. If the AI fails, the system still works.

6. Detection Rule: The Two-Window Design

For each session and each route, count POST submissions in two sliding windows at once. GET requests, other routes and other sessions never add to the count.

Setting Short window (burst) Long window (slow-drip)
Length 5 seconds 60 seconds
Warn at 4 submits 11 submits
Slow at 8 submits 20 submits

Other settings: double-click grace 1 second, amnesty 5 minutes, delay steps 2 s then 4 s. All values are editable in the dashboard.

Decision ladder: a session takes the more severe result of the two windows.

Decision Trigger Effect
Allow Both windows under Warn Passes normally
Warn Either window reaches Warn Passes; student sees a calm notice; operator sees a yellow flag
Slow Either window reaches Slow Accepted and receipted, but processed with a visible delay (2 s, then 4 s) in a separate slow lane

Why two windows: the short window catches rapid-fire bursts. The long window catches a script that spaces requests just under the short limit. Each flag names which window fired.

Decay: after a calm period the delay steps down (4 to 2 to 0) and the session returns to Allow.

7. Fairness Safeguards

  • Distinct-session awareness: many sessions with low counts show "Busy period, all fair"; one session with a high count shows "Flood."
  • Double-click grace: two identical submits within one second count once.
  • Timestamp Guarantee Receipt: every submit is stamped on arrival. Being slowed never changes the recorded submission time, so deadlines are protected.
  • Student-friendly message: "Your draft is saved. You can submit again in 8 s."
  • Queue position: slowed submissions appear in receipt order ("position 3 of 5").
  • Evidence, not triggers: timing gaps and an optional form_opened yes/no flag are shown to explain a flag but never trigger one. The count rule is the only trigger, which avoids unexplained false positives.

8. Standout Features

8.1 Guard OFF vs Guard ON (Impact Benchmark)

A toggle replays the same flood twice through a simulated server with fixed capacity.

Mode What happens Expected result
Guard OFF The flood shares one queue with students and clogs it Student wait times spike, targeting 10+ seconds
Guard ON The flooding session is isolated into a slow lane Student wait times stay flat, targeting under 0.2 seconds

The simulation is clearly labelled and the numbers come out of the model, not hard-coded. A summary line shows average student wait for each mode.

8.2 Traffic Barcode (Visual Timeline)

Each session gets a row, and every submit places a dot along a time axis, colour-coded by decision.

  • Real students: scattered green dots across many rows.
  • Script attack: a dense, solid line on one row, turning yellow then orange.
  • Slow-drip bot: evenly spaced dots, which is visibly different from human scatter.

Judges grasp the difference without reading logs.

8.3 Two-Window Rule

Described in section 6. It demonstrates that the design anticipates evasive behaviour. In the demo, a slow-drip bot slips past the 5-second window but is caught by the 60-second window.

9. Explainability and Operator Tools

  • FLAG_REASON: plain English, for example "15 submits in 3.2 s (short window, Slow at 8), form never opened."
  • Why card: count, window, threshold, a dot timeline of recent requests, and gap evidence ("gaps all about 0.2 s").
  • Clear: resets a session's counts and decision instantly.
  • Grant Amnesty: clears the session and starts a time-boxed 5-minute amnesty during which requests are still counted and shown but cannot trigger Slow. It is never a permanent whitelist, because a permanent bypass is exactly what a script wants.
  • Mistake Recovery Log: records time, session, rule that fired, count, action, who acted (human or AI) and a note. Three or more amnesties on one rule trigger a hint such as "Warn at 4 may be too tight."
  • What-if tuner: change windows or thresholds and re-run the same replay to see how many sessions would be flagged.
  • Fairness scorecard: "Sessions affected: 1 of 34. Legitimate students slowed: 0." An answer key from the generator makes this verifiable.
  • Live graph: Active Sessions, Throttled Sessions and System Health Index. Health is the percentage of active sessions currently in Allow.

10. AI Layer

10.1 Principle

AI assists; the rule engine decides. It is fast, deterministic and explainable at its core, and AI adds language and judgment on top.

10.2 AI roles

Role What it does
Incident Explainer Turns a flag into operator text: "Slow-drip: 11 submits in 60 s, about one every 5 s. The 5 s window never fired; the 60 s window did."
AI Operator Handles routine borderline cases: grants amnesty or recommends it, and logs its reasoning
Benchmark Narrator Summarises the OFF vs ON run: "Student wait fell from 12.4 s to 0.18 s; 1 of 34 sessions throttled."
Threshold Advisor Reads the Mistake Recovery Log and suggests tuning either window
Ask RushGuard (optional) Operator asks "why was S-031 slowed?" and gets a plain answer

10.3 Operator modes

Mode Behaviour
Manual A human decides everything
Suggest AI recommends ("Grant amnesty to S-031?") and a human approves with one tap
Auto AI acts alone within strict limits

10.4 Guardrails

  • The AI may only use existing actions: Clear or the 5-minute Grant Amnesty.
  • It cannot block anyone, change thresholds or alter counts.
  • Amnesty still counts requests, so tricking the AI gains a script nothing real.
  • It forgives only borderline cases (count just over Warn, human-like gaps). Clear floods stay throttled.
  • If unsure, slow or offline, it escalates to a human or falls back to the plain rule.
  • Every AI action is logged with reasoning and can be undone by a human.
  • Metric shown: "AI actions overturned by humans."

10.5 Providers (all free)

Priority Provider Why
Primary Gemini API key from Google AI Studio Free tier with no billing; strongest quality
Backup Groq (not the same as Grok) Free, no card, very fast
Offline fallback Ollama (local model) Fully free and unlimited, lower quality

Free-tier limits change often, so verify them in each console before demo day. The provider is a single config setting so switching takes seconds. Only fictional session summaries are sent. API keys stay on the backend, never in the browser. AI text for the three demo scenarios is cached, with template text as a fallback.

11. How It Works End to End

  1. A request arrives from the replay or mock form with timestamp, session ID, route, method and optional form_opened.
  2. Non-POST requests are logged in history but not counted.
  3. For POSTs, the double-click grace is applied, then the submit is added to the session's recent list.
  4. Entries older than each window are dropped and both windows are counted.
  5. A receipt is stamped with the arrival time.
  6. The more severe window result gives Allow, Warn or Slow, respecting any active amnesty.
  7. Slow requests go to the slow lane with the current delay and a queue position; calm behaviour steps the delay down.
  8. The engine builds the reason string. The AI layer may expand it.
  9. The dashboard updates the table, barcode, graph, scorecard and benchmark.
  10. A human or the AI operator may clear or grant amnesty, which is logged.

18. Limitations and Ethics

  • Session rotation: a script that changes session IDs would evade a per-session rule.
  • Simulated benchmark: it models a server rather than measuring a real one, and is labelled as such.
  • Shared devices: a lab computer could look busy, so thresholds are adjustable and amnesty exists.
  • Privacy by design: only session ID, route, method, timing and a coarse flag are used.
  • Human in the loop: every decision is explainable and overridable, and every override is recorded.
  • Proportionate response: the worst outcome is a short, decaying delay with a guaranteed receipt.
  • AI safety: AI cannot block, cannot change settings, and sees only fictional data.

20. Likely Judge Questions

  • Can a bot just change its session? Yes. That is out of scope; RushGuard targets fairness on a shared service, not distributed attacks.
  • Why not let AI decide? Predictability and explainability matter when a student could be affected, so AI advises and acts only within limits.
  • Are the benchmark numbers real? They come from a labelled simulation with fixed capacity, so the effect is demonstrated, not claimed.
  • How do you avoid punishing students? Distinct-session awareness, double-click grace, gentle delays, receipts and reversible amnesty.
